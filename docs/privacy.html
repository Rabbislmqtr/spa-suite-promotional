The short version

1

Four sentences, if that is all you read.

  • The salon owns its records. We hold them for you. We do not own them, sell them, rent them, or use them for anyone’s marketing — ours included.
  • Your branches are separated in the queries, not just on the screen. A branch login cannot read another branch’s figures, and our own test suite fails if that ever stops being true.
  • Every account has a role, and the log has a name on it. Six roles decide what each person can open, and the audit log records who changed a sale, a salary or a setting.
  • You can leave with everything. Reports export as PDF or spreadsheet, and the whole database comes out as one file you keep.

Who is who

2

Your business is the one answerable for the data.

When a client gives a spa their mobile number, they are giving it to the spa. Under Qatar’s data protection law the salon is the controller of that information: the salon decides why it is held and what happens to it. Spa Suite is a processor — we keep the records on the salon’s instructions, and we decide nothing else about them.

In practice that means the salon answers its own clients’ questions, and we answer the salon’s. Section 9 sets out how we handle it when a customer writes to the wrong one of us.

Staff records — attendance, pay, commission, leave — belong to the salon’s employment relationship with its team, and are held on the same basis.

What is stored

3

The whole list, by kind.

Customers

  • Name and mobile number, and the notes you type yourself (consultation notes, preferences, occasions).
  • Visit history: what was had, who did it, when, and at which branch.
  • Money owed to you and money held with you — dues, credit, overpayment balances — and the collection records behind them.
  • Loyalty stamps and rewards, and if the customer added a card to a phone wallet, the pass identifier and the device registration used to update that pass.
  • Feedback: the rating out of ten and any comment the customer leaves.

Bookings and the diary

  • Appointments, the staff member and room assigned, the services chosen, and the reschedule history.
  • Staff preferences, and any blocking rule you have recorded for a customer.

Money

  • Sales, invoices, line items, discounts, payments, split payment methods, refunds, adjustments and overpayment returns.
  • Expenses by category, drawer opening floats, cash-in records, drawer authorisation, and the counted close of each business day.
  • No card numbers. The till records how much was taken by card; the card itself is handled by your payment terminal and never reaches Spa Suite.

Staff

  • Accounts and roles. Passwords are stored only as one-way hashes — nobody here can read yours, and we will not ask you for it.
  • Attendance and clock-in times, off-day and leave records, overtime, fines, commission, salary figures and resignations.

Running records

  • Branch settings: receipt header, logo and tax number, service list and prices, rooms and staff setup.
  • An audit log of changes, and a system log of sign-ins and administrative actions.
  • Database backups produced by the one-click backup and by the close of the business day.

We do not hold passport, national ID or residence permit documents, and the system has no field for them. If you keep copies of those documents in your own filing, they are not in Spa Suite.

Where it sits

4

Two arrangements. You choose one, in writing, before your first day.

We host it for you

The application and its database run on hosting we provide, located in {{HOSTING_REGION}} with {{HOSTING_PROVIDER}}. Backups of your database are written by the system, as described below. The application contains no analytics, no advertising code and no third-party tracker of any kind, and we add none.

You host it

Spa Suite installed on a machine in your branch — or on hosting you already pay for — keeps every record on hardware you control, over your own network. In that arrangement nothing leaves the building: the database, the backups and the uploaded receipt logo all sit on your disk. We never copy your database anywhere unless you explicitly ask us to move or restore something.

Backups, either way

You can back the whole database up in one click at any time and download the file, and closing the business day prompts a backup as part of the close, so there is a copy from the end of every trading day. Where those files are kept is your decision — we recommend keeping a copy off the machine, and if you would like that automated, that is a hosting arrangement we will set up with you.

Cookies

5

This website sets none. The application sets one.

The pages you are reading now set no cookies at all and run no analytics. They load two typefaces from Google Fonts, which means Google’s servers receive the request as they would for any site using them; we send nothing of our own along with it, and get nothing back about you.

The application, once someone signs in, sets exactly one session cookie. It is marked HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax, and Secure when the system is served over HTTPS, which we recommend and will help you set up. It carries a session identifier and nothing else — no name, no role, no tracking value.

  • A normal session ends after 30 minutes of inactivity, and after 12 hours regardless of activity.
  • The session identifier is rotated every 15 minutes, so a copied cookie goes stale quickly.
  • The terminal account used at reception can stay signed in on that one device for 30 days, refreshed every 6 hours, so the desk is not retyping a password between clients. Changing the password ends it.

No advertising, analytics or third-party cookies are set anywhere in the system.

Who can see it

6

Your team, by role — and us, only when you ask.

Six roles — owner, admin, manager, receptionist, staff and cleaner — decide what each person can open. Reports, payroll and settings are not on a receptionist’s screen, and the check happens on the server as well as in the menu, so a guessed web address gets a refusal rather than a page. On a multi-branch setup, an account is scoped to a branch as well.

Inside your own team the audit log is what makes the rest work: a corrected sale, an adjusted salary or a changed setting has a name and a timestamp against it, and the system log covers sign-ins and administrative actions.

Our own access. We look at a system when you ask us to fix something or answer a question about it, and for nothing else. If a fix would mean reading real customer records rather than the shape of the data, we tell you first and ask you to authorise it. We do not browse your books, and there is no hidden account sitting in your user list.

How it ends

7

Kept while you use it. Handed over when you stop.

  • While you are subscribed, the records are kept for as long as you need them, and you can export or delete any part of them from inside the system at any time.
  • When you cancel, you get the export and the final database file before the switch-off, and we confirm in writing that you have them.
  • After a hosted subscription ends, the database and the backups we hold are deleted within 30 days. If you want that sooner, say so and we will do it that week and confirm it.
  • On a self-hosted install there is nothing for us to delete — the data never left your machine. Deleting it is in your hands, and we will walk you through it if you want.
  • Our own housekeeping copies — a support email that happens to contain a screenshot, for instance — are deleted once the issue is closed, on the same 30 days.

Security

8

What we do, and what is honestly yours to hold.

  • Passwords are stored as one-way hashes, never as text, and never emailed to you.
  • Role and branch checks run on the server for every page and every action, not in the browser.
  • Sessions expire, rotate, and can be ended by changing a password.
  • We recommend HTTPS on every install, and help you put it in place with a certificate.
  • Uploaded files live in a directory the web server is not allowed to execute, and filenames are generated rather than taken from what you type.
  • Every database query is a prepared statement; no SQL is assembled from what a user typed.

Two things we will not pretend about. First, no system can promise to be immune to attack, and we will not write a clause that claims otherwise. Second, on a self-hosted install the physical security of the machine, your network, your operating system updates and your off-site copies are yours to manage — we will advise, but they are not ours to control. On a hosted install they are ours, and this page is the promise.

If a breach ever affects your data, you hear it from us directly, with what was affected and what we did about it, rather than reading it somewhere else first.

Your clients’ rights

9

If one of your clients asks you for their information.

They are entitled to ask what you hold about them, to have it corrected, and to ask you to delete it. Because the salon is the controller, the request belongs with you, and the system is built so you can answer it without us: the customer profile shows their history and notes, and it can be corrected or removed from the same screen.

If a client writes to us instead — and some will, because our details are on this site — we do not answer with their information. We tell them the request has to come from the salon that holds their records, and we tell you it arrived. If you would rather we handled the mechanics, we will, on your written instruction.

Deletion is a real deletion: removing a customer removes their profile and what hangs off it. Lines inside invoices and payment records that a tax or accounting rule requires you to keep are retained for that period, and we will tell you plainly which ones they are rather than claiming a clean sweep.
