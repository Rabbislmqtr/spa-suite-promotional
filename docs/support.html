Getting set up

4

From first message to first sale.

Four steps, in order — each one has to happen before the next. Most spas are ringing up real sales the same week.

Step 1

Fifteen minutes on a call

You show us how the business runs now: services, rooms, staff, how you split cash and card, what the month-end looks like. We show you the system doing it.

Same day, usually

Step 2

We load your real data

Your service list and prices, rooms, staff and their commission rates, branch details and tax number, and the receipt header in English and Arabic. You send a price list; we do the typing.

We do this part

Step 3

Your team gets an hour on the till

Reception learns to book and bill, therapists learn to clock in, and whoever closes the day practises the drawer count on test data before it counts for real.

Training included

Step 4

You open the first real day

Float goes in, the drawer is authorised, and you're live. We stay on WhatsApp through the first close so nobody is guessing at 10pm.

We're on the first close

The eight we get asked most.

Answered as specifically as we can. If the answer you need isn't here, ask us on WhatsApp and we'll add it.

A web browser and a thermal receipt printer. Spa Suite runs in the browser on whatever the desk already has — a desktop, a laptop or a tablet — with nothing to install on each device. Receipts print at 80mm. Chrome is what we test on most.

Every device needs to reach the server the system is installed on. Hosted online, that means an internet connection and you can log in from anywhere — home, another branch, your phone. Installed on a machine in the branch, the tills reach it over your own network. We'll talk through which suits you on the call.

Yes. We run a demo with your own services, rooms and staff loaded in, then ring up a test sale and close a test day so you see the part that matters rather than a slide deck. No card details, no commitment.

Yes — that's the normal way it runs. Reception can be billing while a therapist clocks in and the owner reads yesterday's report, all on different devices. There are six roles — owner, admin, manager, receptionist, staff and cleaner — and each one only sees what it should: what a receptionist can open is not what a manager or the owner can.

Each branch keeps its own cash drawer, staff, rooms, receipt header and tax number, so its day opens and closes on its own. They share one customer database, so a client's history and loyalty stamps follow them between locations. Admins switch branches from the top of the screen, or read consolidated reports across all of them.

Three things. Six roles control what each person can open — reports, payroll and settings are not on a receptionist's screen. An audit log records who changed what, so a corrected sale or an adjusted salary has a name and a timestamp against it. And system logs cover sign-ins and administrative actions. We recommend serving the system over HTTPS, which we'll set up with you.

Two ways, both under your control. You can back the whole database up in one click, any time, and download the file. And closing the business day prompts a backup as part of the close, so there's a copy from the end of every trading day rather than from whenever somebody last remembered. If you want these copied off-site on a schedule, that's a hosting arrangement and we'll set it up with you.

Yes, and we think you should be able to leave with it. Sales reports, monthly statements, attendance, salary slips, expense reports and customer feedback all export as PDFs or spreadsheets. The full database backup is a file you download and keep. Nothing is held back to make cancelling harder.
